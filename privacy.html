Unraid Dash privacy policy

Last updated November 15, 2026.

Unraid Dash connects your Apple device to Unraid servers and web services that you configure. You do not create an Unraid Dash account. The app has no developer-operated telemetry or analytics service, advertising SDK, or tracking SDK, and does not track you across apps or websites. The developer does not automatically receive your server information, credentials, or app logs; the developer receives a feedback report only when you choose to send one.

Information used by the app

  • Server profiles and preferences: server names, addresses, server identifiers, container preferences and web UI address overrides are stored on your device. Profiles and their preferences can sync through Apple's iCloud key-value store using your Apple account. Device settings, including the app lock and route preferences, stay local.
  • Credentials: API keys and saved server and web UI usernames and passwords are stored in Apple's Keychain. App Store builds use synchronizable Keychain items so these credentials can sync through iCloud Keychain when available for your Apple account. Credentials are not stored in the profile preferences or snapshot cache.
  • Cached content: the app keeps the last server snapshot, trusted certificate information and downloaded container icons on your device. Embedded web UIs use a persistent WebKit data store for each server, which can contain cookies, cached pages and other website data.
  • Local diagnostics: the app records actions, state changes and errors in Apple's local unified logging system, writing only the redacted part of each entry. It also keeps a small activity journal of typed events (such as launches, screen changes and errors, without names or addresses) and the crash and freeze reports Apple's MetricKit delivers, in its own storage on this device; the journal holds at most two small files, the oldest replaced first. Diagnostics › Recent log shows, copies and shares the app's state, those reports, the journal and recent log entries, redacted. The app does not upload any of this to the developer on its own; it is included in a feedback report only if you send one with diagnostic info on.

If you enable the app lock or reveal a saved password, the app uses Apple's device authentication interface. Authentication is handled by the operating system; the app receives the result, not your fingerprint or face template.

Connections you choose

The app sends requests directly to the server addresses you configure, including reachable LAN and Tailscale addresses. Requests can include an API key, login information and actions that you choose to perform. Your servers and their administrators control how they handle those requests. The app does not provide a VPN or relay these requests through a developer-operated server.

Web pages opened inside the app can load content and make requests according to the behavior of those services. Their operators' privacy policies apply to information you provide to them.

Load icons from the internet is on by default. When enabled, the app downloads container icons from image URLs supplied by your server. Those hosts receive ordinary network request information, such as your IP address. The icon loader does not use the app's saved credentials or web session cookies. You can turn this setting off in Settings.

Apple handles iCloud, Keychain sync and operating-system diagnostics under your Apple account, device settings and Apple's policies. These services are separate from developer telemetry.

Your controls and retention

Profiles and saved credentials remain available until you change or remove them. Cached content, website data and local system logs can also remain after you close the app. The operating system controls log retention and cache eviction.

You can edit or remove saved logins in Settings. Sign out of all web UIs clears website data for the selected server. Clearing data for a host affects web UIs on that host, including other ports. Deleting a server requests removal of its credentials, certificate pins, snapshot and web data, and removes its profile. A deletion marker containing the profile identifier and deletion time is retained in profile storage to prevent an older synced copy from restoring the server. Removal can be affected by storage errors or sync availability; Apple controls sync and backup retention. Removing an app profile does not delete data or revoke an API key on your Unraid server. Revoke server credentials through the server when needed.

Information you choose to share for support

Send feedback in the app. The app sends a feedback report only when you choose Send after reviewing the whole report; it never sends one on its own. You can open the form from Settings › Help, from the Help menu on a Mac, or by shaking an iPhone or iPad. A report contains what it's about, your message, the screen you were on and how you opened the form (for example "Docker · opened from the Help menu"), and the app version and build. If you leave Include diagnostic info on, it also contains your device model and system version, a few facts about the app's state (such as its version, free disk space, locale and time zone), crash and freeze reports from the last 14 days, the app's activity journal from the last three days, and up to the last hour of Unraid Dash's own log entries. Those entries already hide private values, and the app removes web addresses, IP addresses, local and Tailscale host names, file paths, quoted names, your servers' names and long key-like strings again before building the report. You can also attach a screenshot of the screen you were on. It is off unless you turn it on, isn't offered when you open the form from Settings, and is never taken while the app is locked. A screenshot can show server addresses or web UI content, so check it on the review screen before sending. Credentials are never included.

The report is saved through Apple's CloudKit as a record in the public database of the app's iCloud container, iCloud.com.sequoyahgeber.unraiddash. Despite the name, other people cannot read records there: its security settings let anyone signed in to iCloud create a report, and only the developer can read reports. iCloud stores the record with the time it was saved and an identifier for your iCloud account that is specific to this app; that identifier does not give the developer your name or email address. Apple's App Privacy label lists this identifier as a User ID. Reports are used to investigate and fix problems. Reports are deleted once dealt with, and at the latest 12 months after they are sent. Apple handles the record under its iCloud terms. The app does not keep a copy after sending. If you close the form without sending, the unsent report stays in the app until you send or discard it, or the app quits. If sending fails, Save or share the report hands the same report to your device's share sheet, and where it goes from there is your choice. To have your reports deleted, send another report asking for it, or email support (include the report ID from its first line if you have it). The developer will delete every report you sent from this app.

If you contact support by email, the developer receives the information you choose to send, which may include your email address, a problem description, screenshots or copied diagnostics. It is used to investigate and respond to your request. Email providers also handle that information under their own policies. Review anything you share and remove passwords, API keys, server addresses and personal content. Copying logs puts text on your clipboard; it does not send a support report.

Support reports may be retained with their correspondence history to resolve problems. Contact support about removal of information you supplied; copies held by email providers or others are subject to their own controls and retention.

Contact and changes

For privacy questions or support, send feedback from Settings › Help › Send feedback, or visit Unraid Dash support. Changes to the app's handling of information will be reflected in an updated policy.

These support pages

These static support and privacy pages are hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub handles ordinary connection information when you visit them under GitHub's privacy statement. Visiting these pages does not upload your app logs, server profiles or credentials.
